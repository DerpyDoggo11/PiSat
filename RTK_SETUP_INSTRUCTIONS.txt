================================================================================
 PiSat  --  Two-unit RTK GPS over WiFi  --  FULL SETUP & WIRING GUIDE
================================================================================
 One PiSat is the BASE (fixed, known spot). One PiSat is the ROVER (the thing
 you track / drive). The base computes GPS correction data (RTCM3) and sends it
 to the rover over WiFi. The rover applies the corrections to get a much more
 accurate ("RTK") position, follows a GPS waypoint path, and streams telemetry
 to a Processing sketch on your laptop.

 Everything in this guide matches the firmware in this repo. Both external
 microcontrollers are ESP32-C3 Super Minis:
   RP2350 Firmware/        -> both PiSats (rover = env "gnss", base = env "base")
   ESP32 Firmware/         -> the rover brain, ESP32-C3 Super Mini (env "c3_rover")
   ESP32-C3 Base Firmware/ -> the base WiFi relay, ESP32-C3 Super Mini (env "c3_base")
   Processing/PiSatRTK/    -> the laptop visualiser

 0. WHAT YOU NEED
================================================================================
  - 2x assembled PiSat boards, each with a GPS antenna on the SMA connector.
    NOTE: the PiSat's ONBOARD ESP32-C3 is NOT populated on these boards, and it
    is not needed. All WiFi runs on the two EXTERNAL ESP32-C3 super minis (which
    have their own PCB antennas). The PiSat's 2.4 GHz WiFi antenna connector is
    therefore unused -- you don't need to fit it. Nothing is ever flashed
    "through" the PiSat; each external C3 and each PiSat RP2350 is flashed over
    its own USB port.
  - 2x ESP32-C3 Super Mini   (one = ROVER brain, one = BASE relay)
  - 2x ZS-X111H (a.k.a. ZS-X11H) brushless motor controllers + 2 BLDC motors +
    a motor battery (6-60 V per the driver). Optional for bench testing -- the
    whole GPS/RTK/telemetry chain works with no motors connected.
  - Dupont wires, common ground between each PiSat and its ESP32.
  - A laptop with Processing 4.x installed, with WiFi.
  - USB cables to flash each board.

  Logic levels: RP2350, ESP32 and ESP32-C3 GPIO are all 3.3 V. You can wire them
  directly -- no level shifter needed.

================================================================================
 1. HOW RTK WORKS HERE
================================================================================
  Normal GPS is accurate to a few metres. RTK (Real-Time Kinematic) puts a
  second receiver (the BASE) at a fixed point. Because the base is not moving,
  it can measure the tiny per-satellite errors (atmosphere, clocks, orbits) that
  a nearby rover sees too. It packages those errors as standard RTCM3 correction
  messages. The rover subtracts them and can resolve its position far more
  precisely.

  In this project the RTCM3 corrections travel BASE -> WiFi -> ROVER:
    - The base PiSat's GNSS is put in "base" mode and emits RTCM3.
    - The base's ESP32-C3 relays that stream over WiFi to the rover.
    - The rover's ESP32-C3 (a transparent WiFi<->UART bridge) pushes it into the
      rover PiSat's RP2350, which feeds it to the GNSS.
    - The rover GNSS outputs its improved fix; the fix "quality" field tells you
      what you got: 1 = plain GPS, 2 = DGPS, 5 = RTK float, 4 = RTK fixed.

  WHO DOES WHAT ON THE ROVER: the PiSat RP2350 is the brain -- it parses the
  GNSS, applies the RTK corrections, follows the waypoint path, and DRIVES the
  two ZS-X111H motor controllers (which are wired to the PiSat's own breakout
  GPIO). The rover's ESP32-C3 only carries WiFi: RTCM in, telemetry out.

  >> Accuracy note: the PiSat GNSS is a Lantronix PNT-SG3FS (ST Teseo III),
     a SINGLE-FREQUENCY (L1) module. It genuinely accepts RTCM corrections
     (DGPS per RTCM 10402.3) and can output carrier-phase measurements, so you
     WILL get a large accuracy improvement (typically sub-metre / DGPS, and L1
     RTK under good sky view). But it is not a dual-band ZED-F9P: expect longer
     time-to-fix and less robust centimetre "RTK FIXED" than dual-band gear.
     See section 13 for how to confirm/enable the exact receiver mode.

 2. SYSTEM / DATA-FLOW DIAGRAM
================================================================================

   BASE SIDE (fixed tripod)                 ROVER SIDE (the robot)
   ------------------------                 ----------------------
   [GPS antenna]                            [GPS antenna]
        |                                        |
   +----------+                     +----------+  GNSS UART (both ways)
   | PiSat    | GNSS UART           | PiSat    |  Teseo III
   | (BASE)   | Teseo III           | (ROVER)  |<--- RTCM3 in / GGA RTK out
   | RP2350   |---> RTCM3 framed    | RP2350   |  BRAIN: GNSS+RTK+pathfinding
   +----------+                     +----+-----+---------+-------------------+
        | GP0(TX)                GP0(TX) |    | GP1(RX)  | J5/J6 breakout:
        v                     telemetry  |    | RTCM in  | 2x PWM/DIR/BRAKE+M
   +--------------+           +----------+    +--------+ v  ("ESP32" header UART)
   | ESP32-C3     | WiFi STA  | ESP32-C3 SM (WiFi BRIDGE)| +------------------+
   | Super Mini   |==========>| SoftAP "PiSat-RTK"       | | 2x ZS-X111H BLDC |
   | (relay)      | TCP :2101 | :2101 RTCM in (->RP2350) | |  -> 2 wheels     |
   +--------------+  (RTCM)   | :8888 telemetry (<-RP2350)| +------------------+
                              +-------------+------------+
                                    ^       | TCP :8888 telemetry
                       WiFi (join   |       v
                       PiSat-RTK)  +-----------------+
                                   | Laptop / Processing
                                   +-----------------+

   The ROVER PiSat (RP2350) is the brain: it applies the RTK corrections,
   follows the path, and drives the two ZS-X111H controllers on its own
   breakout GPIO. The rover's ESP32-C3 is only the access point + a transparent
   WiFi<->UART bridge: RTCM in to the RP2350, telemetry out to Processing.

================================================================================
 3. PiSat BOARD PINOUT REFERENCE
================================================================================
 (Source: schematic.png, wiring.png, and RP2350 Firmware/src/pins.h. The RP2350
  is the hub: it owns the GNSS, the onboard ESP32-C3, and all breakout headers.)

 3.1  RP2350  <->  onboard GNSS (Teseo III / PNT-SG3FS)   -- already routed on PCB
      RP2350 GP4  = UART TX  -> GNSS UART_RX
      RP2350 GP5  = UART RX  <- GNSS UART_TX     (Serial2 @ 9600 baud default)
      RP2350 GP6  = GNSS NRESET
      RP2350 GP7  = GNSS WAKEUP
      RP2350 GP8  = GNSS PPS (1 pulse/sec, rising-edge IRQ)
      RP2350 GP9  = GNSS ANTOFF (antenna power / LNA enable, active behaviour
                    in firmware: LOW = antenna on)
      The GPS RF path: SMA connector -> RF_IN -> module. Put the GPS antenna
      with clear sky view; keep it away from the WiFi antenna.

 3.2  RP2350  <->  onboard "ESP32" header  -- the C3 footprint is NOT POPULATED,
      so this hardware UART (Serial1) is REUSED as the external-C3 link:
      RP2350 GP0 = TX (-> external C3 RX)   RP2350 GP2 = (was C3 EN) unused
      RP2350 GP1 = RX (<- external C3 TX)   RP2350 GP3 = (was C3 BOOT) unused
      This is the firmware default (PISAT_LINK_HW_UART = 1). It is the same
      6-pin "ESP32" header used for power/GND (sec. 3.5).

 3.3  Status / HMI
      RP2350 GP10 = LED A (status)   active-LOW
      RP2350 GP11 = LED B (status)   active-LOW
      RP2350 GP25 = Buzzer

 3.4  Breakout headers (where you attach the external ESP32-C3 and the motors)
      J4  (Conn_01x03, "GPIO 27/28/29"):  GP27, GP28, GP29  -- ADC-capable, spare
      J5  (Conn_01x05, "GPIO 20-24"):      GP20, GP21, GP22, GP23, GP24
           ^ ROVER: RIGHT-wheel ZS-X111H signals (GP24 spare):
               GP20 = RIGHT PWM(P)   GP21 = RIGHT SPEED(M fb)   GP22 = RIGHT BRAKE
               GP23 = RIGHT DIR(ZF)  GP24 = spare
      J6  (Conn_01x05):                    GP12, GP13, GP14, GP15, GP19
           ^ ROVER: LEFT-wheel ZS-X111H signals (GP14 spare). GP12/GP13 are free
             because the C3 link is on the "ESP32" header (GP0/GP1):
               GP12 = LEFT ROTATION(ZF)   GP13 = LEFT BRAKE   GP14 = spare
               GP15 = LEFT PWM(P)         GP19 = LEFT SPEED(M fb)
           NOTE: confirm which physical pin is GP12 vs GP13 on your silkscreen /
           the KiCad netlist. The firmware (pins.h: PIN_J6[5]={12,13,14,15,19})
           treats them as J6 positions 1..5.

 3.5  Debug / power / link pads
      "RP2350" header: 3V3, GND, CLK(SWCLK), DIO(SWDIO), RUN
      "ESP32"  header: RX, TX, EN, BOOT, 3V3, GND
         ^ This is now the LINK header to the external C3. Its two data pins are
           RP2350 GP0 (TX, drives the C3 RX) and GP1 (RX, reads the C3 TX), plus
           3V3 + GND to power/ground the C3. The silk "RX"/"TX" are named for the
           absent onboard C3, so don't take them at face value -- if data doesn't
           flow, swap the two signal wires. EN/BOOT are unused.

 3.6  Power
      RP2350 GP26 = VBAT ADC (2:1 divider, 3.3 V ref) -> battery voltage
      18650 cell -> onboard charger (USB-C) + 3.3 V regulator. ~12 h runtime.

================================================================================
 4. WIRING A:  ROVER  --  PiSat <-> C3 bridge, and PiSat <-> two ZS-X111H
================================================================================
 The rover PiSat (RP2350) is the brain and drives the motors from its OWN
 breakout GPIO. The ESP32-C3 Super Mini is only a WiFi bridge. So there are two
 wiring groups: the C3<->PiSat link, and the PiSat<->motors.

 4.1  PiSat rover  <->  C3 Super Mini  (UART link, common GND):
   Use the PiSat's onboard "ESP32" header -- it is the RP2350's real hardware
   UART (Serial1), free because the onboard C3 is not populated. (This is the
   firmware default; it also frees J6 GP12/GP13.)
   PiSat (ROVER) "ESP32" header  ESP32-C3 Super Mini    meaning
   ----------------------------  -------------------    -------
   GP0  (TX, header)  ---->  GPIO 20 (RX pad)           telemetry text out
   GP1  (RX, header)  <----  GPIO 21 (TX pad)           RTCM3 corrections in
   3V3  (header)      ---->  3V3 pin (power the C3)      optional
   GND  (header)      -----  GND                         common ground
   - The header's EN/BOOT pins are unused -- leave them open.
   - Electrically: GP0 is the RP2350 TX (drives the C3 RX); GP1 is the RP2350 RX
     (reads the C3 TX). If telemetry doesn't flow, swap the two data wires (a
     UART TX/RX swap is harmless to try).
   - Link baud 115200 (both sides set).
   - Prefer the J6 soft-UART on GP12(TX)/GP13(RX) instead? Rebuild the rover with
     -DPISAT_LINK_HW_UART=0 and wire those pins as the previous revision did.

 4.2  PiSat rover breakout  <->  two ZS-X111H controllers:
   The motors connect ONLY to the PiSat. Four wires per wheel: P (PWM speed, an
   output), ZF (rotation/direction, output), BRAKE (output), and M (speed
   feedback pulse, an INPUT the PiSat counts). Because the C3 link now uses the
   "ESP32" header, GP12/GP13 are free for motors.

   Wheel  Signal              PiSat GPIO      ZS-X111H terminal
   -----  ------              ----------      -----------------
   RIGHT  pwm                 GP20 (J5)  ->   P     (PWM speed input)   [driver #1]
   RIGHT  speed feedback      GP21 (J5)  <-   M     (FG / pulse output) [driver #1]
   RIGHT  brake               GP22 (J5)  ->   BRAKE                     [driver #1]
   RIGHT  rotation (dir)      GP23 (J5)  ->   ZF    (direction)         [driver #1]
   LEFT   pwm                 GP15 (J6)  ->   P     (PWM speed input)   [driver #2]
   LEFT   speed feedback      GP19 (J6)  <-   M     (FG / pulse output) [driver #2]
   LEFT   brake               GP13 (J6)  ->   BRAKE                     [driver #2]
   LEFT   rotation (dir)      GP12 (J6)  ->   ZF    (direction)         [driver #2]
   Signal ground (both)       GND (pad)  ->   G     (each driver's P/G header)

   >> LEFT brake on GP13, LEFT rotation on GP12.
      NOTE: these LEFT pins only work with the default HW-UART link. If you
      rebuild with -DPISAT_LINK_HW_UART=0, GP12/GP13 revert to the link and the
      firmware refuses to compile until you move LEFT brake/rotation.

   Driver-to-motor (NOT through the PiSat):
     B+ / B-      -> motor battery (6-60 V). Do NOT power the PiSat from B+.
     MA / MB / MC -> motor phase wires
     H            -> motor hall-sensor connector (sensored motors)

   Motor-supply ground and the PiSat signal ground MUST be tied together
   (common GND) so the PWM/DIR/BRAKE levels are referenced correctly.

   Speed-feedback (M) caveat: the firmware enables an internal pull-up and counts
   falling edges, which suits an open-collector M output. If your ZS-X111H drives
   M as a push-pull 5 V signal, drop it to 3.3 V (divider or level shifter) before
   GP21/GP19. It is optional -- unwired, wheel-speed just reads 0 Hz.

 4.3  IMPORTANT ZS-X111H setup (do this or the P input is ignored):
   - PWM MODE: these boards ship set up for their onboard potentiometer. To take
     an external PWM on P/G you must enable the board's "short interface" (the
     solder short / jumper next to the P/G header) AND turn the onboard speed
     potentiometer to MINIMUM. Then P/G accepts PWM: amplitude 2.5-5 V,
     frequency 50 Hz-20 kHz. (The firmware's analogWrite PWM ~1 kHz is in range.)
   - DIRECTION (ZF) and BRAKE are level inputs; BRAKE engages on a HIGH (~5 V).
   - 3.3 V LOGIC CAVEAT: the PiSat RP2350 drives 3.3 V. PWM (min 2.5 V) works
     fine. DIR and BRAKE expect ~5 V high -- 3.3 V USUALLY triggers them, but if
     a wheel won't change direction or brake, add a simple NPN/MOSFET level
     shifter (or a logic-level converter) on ZF/BRAKE. If a wheel spins the WRONG
     way, just flip DIR_R_FORWARD / DIR_L_FORWARD in RP2350 Firmware/src/main.cpp
     -- no rewiring.
   - The M (speed-feedback / FG pulse) output is optional and NOT used by the
     control loop; leave it unconnected.

   Power the C3 from its own USB while bench-testing, or from the PiSat "ESP32"
   header 3V3/GND pads on the robot -- always keeping a common ground. The motor
   drivers run from their own battery (B+/B-), never from the PiSat rail.

================================================================================
 5. WIRING B:  BASE  PiSat  <->  ESP32-C3 Super Mini   (WiFi relay)
================================================================================
 The base PiSat runs the RP2350 "base" firmware: it configures its GNSS for
 RTCM3 output and streams the framed RTCM3 out on the onboard "ESP32" header's
 TX (GP0). The C3 Super Mini reads it and relays it over WiFi. One-way (base->C3).

   PiSat (BASE) "ESP32" header   ESP32-C3 Super Mini           meaning
   ---------------------------   -------------------           -------
   GP0  (TX, header)     ---->  GPIO 4 (RX)                  RTCM3 stream in
   3V3  (header)         ---->  3V3 pin (power the C3)       optional
   GND  (header)         -----  GND                          common ground

   (No return wire needed. GPIO 5 on the C3 is an unused spare. Build the base
    with -DPISAT_LINK_HW_UART=0 to stream on J6 GP12 instead.)

   Power the C3 from its own USB, or from the base PiSat's 3V3/GND pads. The base
   PiSat's onboard C3 is not populated; the RTK WiFi is entirely this external
   Super Mini's own antenna -- keep it out in the open, away from metal.

   Put the base's GPS antenna somewhere fixed with a wide-open view of the sky
   and DON'T move it once it has surveyed its position.

================================================================================
 6. INSTALLING THE TOOLCHAIN (PlatformIO)
================================================================================
  1. Install VS Code, then the "PlatformIO IDE" extension. (Or use the CLI:
     `pip install platformio`.)
  2. First build will auto-download the toolchains. The RP2350 uses the
     earlephilhower arduino-pico core (already pinned in its platformio.ini);
     the ESP32s use the espressif32 platform.

  In the commands below, `pio` is the PlatformIO CLI. In VS Code you can instead
  pick the environment in the status bar and hit Build/Upload.

================================================================================
 7. FLASHING ALL FOUR FIRMWARES
================================================================================
  Flash each board over its own USB. Do them in this order.

  (a) ROVER PiSat -- RP2350 rover firmware:
        cd "RP2350 Firmware"
        pio run -e gnss -t upload
      Hold BOOTSEL if needed for the first flash (RP2350 mass-storage mode).

  (b) BASE PiSat -- RP2350 base firmware:
        cd "RP2350 Firmware"
        pio run -e base -t upload
      (Same code base, -DPISAT_BASE selected by the "base" env.)

  (c) ROVER ESP32-C3 Super Mini -- the WiFi bridge:
        cd "ESP32 Firmware"
        pio run -e c3_rover -t upload

  (d) BASE ESP32-C3 Super Mini -- the WiFi relay:
        cd "ESP32-C3 Base Firmware"
        pio run -e c3_base -t upload

  (If a C3 super mini won't enter download mode: hold BOOT, tap RST, release BOOT,
   then upload.)

  All four are flashed over their OWN USB ports -- nothing goes "through" the
  PiSat. The onboard ESP32-C3 is not populated, so the RP2350 firmware skips its
  onboard-C3 UART bridge (PISAT_ONBOARD_ESP defaults to 0 in main.cpp; the esp_*
  console commands are disabled). If you ever do solder the onboard C3, rebuild
  the rover with -DPISAT_ONBOARD_ESP=1 to re-enable it.

================================================================================
 8. CONFIGURING IT
================================================================================
 8.1  WiFi network (must match on rover C3 bridge and base C3):
        ESP32 Firmware/src/main.cpp           -> AP_SSID / AP_PASS
        ESP32-C3 Base Firmware/src/main.cpp   -> ROVER_SSID / ROVER_PASS
      Defaults: SSID "PiSat-RTK", password "pisat-rtk-1234". The rover C3 is the
      access point at 192.168.4.1. The base C3 and your laptop both join it.

 8.2  The rover's path -- now on the PiSat (RP2350 Firmware/src/main.cpp, in the
      ROVER branch, near the top):
        WAYPOINTS[] : list your field's GPS points {lat, lon}, in drive order
                      (at least 2). Uncomment and replace the placeholders.
        BOUNDARY[]  : optional safety polygon; if the rover leaves it, it stops.
                      Leave < 3 points to disable.
      Coordinates are plain decimal degrees, e.g. { 40.0011234, -75.0022345 }.
      Tip: stand at each corner with the rover, read the "gps" value on the
      RP2350 USB console, write it down. Rebuild/flash the rover (env "gnss").

 8.3  Drive tuning (RP2350 Firmware/src/main.cpp, ROVER branch):
        MAX_PWM, STRAIGHT_DEG, PIVOT_DEG, TURN_FACTOR, LOOKAHEAD_FRAC,
        WAYPOINT_RADIUS_M, MIN_SPEED_FOR_COURSE. Direction/brake polarity:
        DIR_R_FORWARD, DIR_L_FORWARD, BRAKE_ON.
      NOTE: motor output starts conservative (MAX_PWM = 40 / 255). Raise it once
      you trust the steering. Type "hold" on the RP2350 console to brake/release
      the motors while bench-testing. With no motors wired, it's harmless.

 8.4  Base survey-in (RP2350 Firmware/src/gnss.cpp -> Gnss::configureBase()):
        The base does a 60 s / 2.0 m "survey-in" to fix its own coordinates,
        then emits RTCM3. Change the survey time/accuracy there if you want a
        tighter base. See section 13 about the exact ST commands.

================================================================================
 9. BRING-UP / OPERATING SEQUENCE
================================================================================
  1. Set up the BASE first, outdoors, antenna fixed and sky-open. Power the base
     PiSat + its C3. The base PiSat LED-A blinks until it is emitting RTCM
     frames, then goes solid. (USB-serial "stat" prints "rtcm frames out".)
  2. Power the ROVER PiSat + its C3 bridge. The C3 brings up the "PiSat-RTK"
     SoftAP (its USB-serial prints the AP IP and "RTCM server / telemetry
     server"). The PiSat RP2350 USB-serial prints "=== PiSat RTK ROVER ===".
  3. The base C3 auto-joins "PiSat-RTK" and connects to the rover C3's :2101.
     The rover C3 serial prints "base station connected (RTCM)"; corrections now
     flow through it into the PiSat RP2350 and on to the GNSS.
  4. Give the rover GPS an open-sky view. Watch the fix quality climb (on the
     RP2350 "gps"/"stat" console, or in Processing):
     NONE -> GPS -> DGPS -> RTK float -> RTK fixed (best case for this module).
  5. On your laptop, join "PiSat-RTK", then run the Processing sketch (sec. 10).
  6. To actually drive: wire the motors, make sure WAYPOINTS[] is set, and power
     the rover in the field. It only drives when it has a fix and a valid path;
     it stops at the last waypoint or if it leaves the BOUNDARY.

================================================================================
 10. RUNNING THE PROCESSING TELEMETRY VISUALISER
================================================================================
  1. Open Processing/PiSatRTK/PiSatRTK.pde in Processing 4.x.
  2. Confirm ROVER_HOST = "192.168.4.1" and ROVER_PORT = 8888 at the top.
  3. Join the "PiSat-RTK" WiFi on the laptop.
  4. Press Run. The sketch:
       - connects to the rover's telemetry server (auto-reconnects if dropped),
       - draws the waypoint path, the optional boundary polygon, waypoint radii,
       - draws the rover moving along the path with a heading line (its GPS
         course) and a target-bearing line, plus a green breadcrumb trail,
       - shows a live HUD: fix quality (colour-coded: green = RTK fixed), lat/lon
         to 7 decimals, satellites, HDOP, speed, course, current segment, target
         bearing, cross-track heading error, path status, RTCM correction age
         (link health), and per-wheel PWM bars.
     The view auto-fits to the path + rover; a scale bar shows metres.

  No fix yet? The field stays empty until the rover reports its first valid fix
  (that sets the local-frame origin). The HUD still shows "NO FIX" and sat count.

================================================================================
 11. TELEMETRY & CORRECTION PROTOCOL REFERENCE
================================================================================
 11.1  ROVER telemetry: RP2350 -> C3 bridge -> Processing. The RP2350 emits
        newline-terminated ASCII on GP12; the C3 relays it verbatim to the
        Processing TCP client on :8888.
        M,<maxPwm>,<wpRadius>,<wpCount>,<boundaryCount>     (path header, every 2 s)
        W,<i>,<lat>,<lon>                                   (each waypoint)
        B,<i>,<lat>,<lon>                                   (each boundary vertex)
        O,<lat>,<lon>                                       (local-frame origin)
        T,<ms>,<fixQual>,<lat>,<lon>,<course>,<speed>,<sats>,<hdop>,<seg>,
          <tgtBearing>,<hdgErr>,<done>,<rtcmAgeMs>,<pwmR>,<pwmL>,<spdHzR>,<spdHzL>  (10 Hz)
        fixQual: 0 none, 1 GPS, 2 DGPS, 4 RTK fixed, 5 RTK float.
        spdHzR/spdHzL = wheel-speed feedback pulse frequency (Hz).
        (The header is resent every 2 s so a late-joining Processing client still
        gets the path.)

 11.2  Corrections: Processing-side none. Base -> rover is framed RTCM3 (0xD3,
        10-bit length header, payload, 3-byte CRC24Q); the base only forwards
        complete frames. The rover C3 receives them on TCP :2101 and writes the
        raw bytes to the RP2350 (GP13), which passes them into the GNSS UART.

================================================================================
 12. TROUBLESHOOTING
================================================================================
  Rover C3 never says "base station connected":
    - Base C3 and rover C3 SSID/password must match exactly.
    - Rover C3 is the AP (192.168.4.1). Base C3 is STA. Check the base C3 USB
      serial for "joined" then "rover link up".
    - Keep both WiFi antennas in the open; 2.4 GHz hates metal enclosures.

  Motors don't move / wrong direction:
    - Do the ZS-X111H PWM-mode setup (sec. 4.3): short interface + pot to MIN.
    - Type "hold" on the RP2350 console to release the safety hold if engaged.
    - Wrong way? flip DIR_R_FORWARD / DIR_L_FORWARD (sec. 8.3). No fix or no path
      = motors stay stopped by design.
    - DIR/BRAKE not responding at 3.3 V? add a level shifter (sec. 4.3).

  Fix quality never rises above "GPS":
    - Corrections aren't reaching the GNSS. Check RTCM age in the HUD: if it says
      "no corrections", the WiFi/UART chain is broken (see wiring sec. 4/5).
    - The base needs a good sky view and must finish survey-in before it emits
      useful RTCM. Give it a few minutes, don't move it.
    - Confirm the base RP2350 "stat" shows rtcm frames increasing.
    - See section 13: the exact receiver-mode commands may need adjusting.

  Processing window is empty:
    - Laptop must be on "PiSat-RTK". Try http nothing -- it's raw TCP.
    - No origin until the first valid fix. Watch the HUD sat count.

  GNSS debug commands (type them on the RP2350 USB serial, ROVER and BASE both):
    gps  (or sats) -> friendly status: fix quality, satellites tracked, HDOP,
                      1PPS count, and -- before a fix -- how close it is and what
                      to check. Watch sats climb and HDOP fall as it converges.
    stat           -> one-line summary (adds RTCM frames on the base).
    nmea           -> toggle raw NMEA echo (see the actual $GxGGA/$GxRMC stream).
    gnss_reset     -> restart + reconfigure the module.
    ant on | ant off (rover) -> antenna bias power.

  How long to get a fix?
    Clear sky, good antenna: cold start ~30 s-2 min, warm ~20-30 s, hot a few s.
    Indoors/near a window it may take many minutes or never fix. If after a few
    minutes outdoors "gps" still shows 0 sats and "1pps 0", suspect the antenna
    or its bias (below), not the software.

  GNSS shows nothing / never tracks satellites:
    - Default GNSS baud is 9600 (pins.h GNSS_BAUD). Use "nmea" to echo raw NMEA
      and confirm the module is even talking.
    - "ant on", check the SMA antenna, "gnss_reset" to restart the module.
    - Bias-tee inductor NOT populated? That inductor feeds DC up the coax to
      power an ACTIVE antenna's LNA. Without it:
         * PASSIVE antenna (no LNA): still works -- you'll get a fix.
         * ACTIVE antenna (most GPS antennas, incl. the BOM ones): the LNA is
           unpowered -> little/no signal -> likely NO fix. Populate the inductor,
           or use a passive antenna. Test: with the antenna on and "ant on",
           measure DC on the SMA centre pin -- it should be ~3.3 V; 0 V means the
           bias path (that inductor) is open. "1pps 0" in "gps" is the same clue.

  RP2350 won't take new firmware:
    - Enter BOOTSEL (mass storage) and drag the UF2, or let PlatformIO reset it.

  Motors spin unexpectedly:
    - The rover only drives with a valid fix AND >=2 waypoints. Clear WAYPOINTS[]
      to keep it stationary while testing GPS/telemetry.

 13. THE GNSS-MODULE RTK CAVEAT   (READ THIS)
================================================================================
  The PiSat GNSS is a Lantronix PNT-SG3FS = ST Teseo III, SINGLE-FREQUENCY (L1),
  flash-based, multi-constellation (GPS/GLONASS/Galileo/BeiDou/QZSS). Confirmed
  from the datasheet: it supports DGPS corrections per RTCM 10402.3 and can be
  built/configured to output carrier-phase measurements for a host RTK algorithm.

  What that means for THIS project:
    - The transport in this firmware is correct and complete: base -> WiFi ->
      rover -> GNSS, both directions, framed and checksummed.
    - The exact commands that switch the Teseo III into "emit RTCM3 base output"
      and "consume RTCM3 as a rover" are ST $PSTM proprietary NMEA commands. The
      placeholders in gnss.cpp (Gnss::configureBase / configureRover) use the
      $PSTMSETPAR / $PSTMSAVEPAR scheme, but the exact PARAMETER IDs and the
      base-survey command name MUST be verified against YOUR module's:
          "Teseo III / Teseo-LIV3 Software Command / Firmware Reference"
      (ST doc; also see Lantronix's PNT-SG3FS material and ST's TESEO-SUITE PC
      tool, which can configure the module and show you the exact commands).
    - Once the receiver is genuinely in base/rover mode, everything else here
      works unchanged. If your particular firmware build does not expose on-chip
      RTK, you can still (a) get DGPS/sub-metre from RTCM, or (b) log raw
      measurements from both units and post-process with RTKLIB.
    - Realistic expectation for an L1 module: fast to sub-metre/DGPS; L1-only
      RTK "float/fixed" is possible with open sky and a good base but is slower
      and less robust than a dual-band (L1/L2) receiver like the u-blox ZED-F9P.

  So: this repo gives you a working, honest end-to-end RTK pipeline over WiFi;
  the one thing to nail down against ST's docs is the precise receiver-mode
  command set for your exact module firmware.

    - Lantronix PNT-SG3FS datasheet (PDS-0021):
